# Fundraising (FR) field guide

Fundraising is how an organization secures and diversifies the money it needs: a base of supporters, a mix of restricted and flexible funds, multi-year commitments and a plan. Heavy reliance on short-term, restricted grants is the most common exposure for CVI organizations, and in 2025 Fundraising was one of the domains most closely tied to overall capacity (2025 OCI cohort findings).

## By stage

### Seeding and Emerging: build a base

- Start a simple list of every funder, donor and in-kind supporter, with amounts and renewal dates. This gives you a first view of the next 6 to 12 months (OCI questionnaire, Fundraising).
- Look past grants to the people who already know your work: residents, faith communities and local businesses. Leaders recommend broadening the base to include hospitals, legislators, philanthropy and local businesses (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).
- Ask funders for at least some multi-year or general operating support, and explain why stability protects staff and participants.

### Developing: balance and diversify

- Aim for a balance of restricted and flexible funding, and for funders that support operations as well as programs (OCI questionnaire, Fundraising).
- Use performance measures, evaluation findings, storytelling and culturally literate messaging to show your value to the community and to funders (BJA CVI Implementation Checklist, Step 6.1).
- Tell your story with numbers and with people. Leaders describe telling their impact, talking about dollars saved and then bringing in political champions (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).
- Count the cost of grant chasing. Searching, applying and meeting each funder's reporting rules pulls attention from the mission (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).

### Strengthening and Transformational: plan for sustainability

- Write a fundraising or resource development plan with the board, tied to your strategic plan, and give someone dedicated time to carry it out (BJA Checklist, Step 6.1; OCI questionnaire, Fundraising).
- Explore public financing. Some states have created Medicaid reimbursement for CVI services, which depends on provider certification, billing structures and strong service documentation (Ranjan et al., 2025, cited in HVIP focus group brief, 2026).
- Build reserves and multi-year funding so a single lost grant does not threaten staff jobs.
- Prepare for the "success trap." When violence goes down, funders may pull back. Lead with cost comparisons and evidence that sustained investment protects the gains (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).

## Guardrails for honest messaging

- Do not claim savings just because violence declined. Return-on-investment claims need a credible estimate of program impact (Dr. Roman, cost evaluation and ROI).
- Keep participant stories consent-based and dignity-first. Share only what people agree to share, and avoid details that could identify them.

## Team conversation starters

- What share of our budget comes from our single largest funder?
- Which relationships could become multi-year or flexible support this year?
- What story and what numbers best show the difference we make?
