# Financial Management (FM) field guide

Financial Management is how an organization tracks, controls and reports its money: an accounting system, budgets, internal controls, audits, reserves and transparent reporting to leaders and the board. Strong systems protect your mission, your staff and your relationships with funders. In 2025 this domain was closely tied to overall capacity, alongside Planning and Fundraising (2025 OCI cohort findings).

## By stage

### Seeding and Emerging: get the basics in place

- Use an accounting system, even a simple one, and record every transaction the same way (OCI questionnaire, Financial Management).
- Share financial duties so no one person handles everything. For example, one person approves payments and another records them (OCI questionnaire, Financial Management).
- Build an annual budget and protect payroll first. Steady pay is a foundation for staff trust (OCI questionnaire, Financial Management).

### Developing: controls, reports and real costs

- Compare actual spending to the budget every month and explain the differences.
- Produce an income statement and balance sheet on a regular schedule and review them as a leadership team (OCI questionnaire, Financial Management).
- Complete an independent financial review or audit, and use the findings to improve.
- Budget the full cost of your work. Realistic costing includes supervision, administration, data, training, transportation, crisis supports and indirect costs, not only frontline salaries (HVIP focus group brief, 2026; Everytown and The HAVI costing guide, 2024).
- Know your cost per participant, including the cost of serving someone for a full year at the intended level of service (Dr. Roman, cost evaluation).

### Strengthening and Transformational: plan ahead and be transparent

- Track spending by program and funding stream so you can report accurately to each funder (OCI questionnaire, Financial Management).
- Develop multi-year budgets and projections that match your strategic plan.
- Build reserves to ride out late payments or a lost grant.
- Have the board review financial reports regularly and approve the budget each year (IRS, Governance and Related Topics).
- File taxes and the Form 990 accurately and on time, and complete audits promptly after the fiscal year closes.
- If your organization is larger and well established, consider passing flexible funds to smaller, less resourced partners (BJA CVI Implementation Checklist, Step 3.2).

## Practical tips

- Strong documentation is protection. When one grantee's problems lead to extra scrutiny for everyone, organizations with clean records spend less time responding (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).
- Build practical supports into the budget. One program used mileage reimbursement so staff could drive participants to appointments, which improved engagement (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).
- If Medicaid reimbursement may come to your state, start improving service documentation and coding now (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).

## Team conversation starters

- Do we know what it truly costs to serve one participant for a year?
- Who reviews our finances, and how often?
- How many months could we operate if our largest grant ended?
