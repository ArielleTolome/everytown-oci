# Monitoring, Learning and Evaluation (ML) field guide

Monitoring, learning and evaluation is how an organization tracks its work, learns from it and shows its impact. The goal is learning, not paperwork: what gets measured gets done, and success that is not seen cannot be learned from or rewarded (Dr. Roman, What Makes a Good CVI Program). Many organizations in the 2025 cohort already had strong practices here to build on (2025 OCI cohort findings).

## Evaluation readiness stages

Dr. Roman describes three stages of measurement and evaluation readiness (Dr. Roman, evaluation readiness):

1. **Laying the foundation:** set up data capture and develop a logic model with defined measures.
2. **Measuring performance:** measures are defined and collected systematically.
3. **Ready to evaluate for impact:** an evaluation partner is in place.

Most organizations move through these in order, and each stage is valuable on its own.

## By OCI stage

### Seeding and Emerging: count what you do

- Track basic activities: people served, contacts, mediations, referrals and services delivered (OCI questionnaire, Monitoring, Learning and Evaluation).
- Write down two or three outcomes you hope to see, linked to your logic model. Every program should have performance measures that match its logic model (Dr. Roman, performance monitoring).
- Track inputs, activities and outputs first; these become the building blocks for later evaluation (Dr. Roman, performance monitoring).

### Developing: make data a team habit

- Use the same tools and definitions every time so data stays consistent, and train staff to use them (OCI questionnaire, Monitoring, Learning and Evaluation).
- Review data together as a team and use it to adjust programs (BJA CVI Implementation Checklist, Step 5.1).
- Ask community members to help define indicators of success, and measure program quality, not only volume (BJA Checklist, Step 5.1).
- Move from tracking whatever each funder asks for to one cumulative data system you own. Leaders say this made reporting easier and helped them tell their story with numbers (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).

### Strengthening and Transformational: evaluate and share

- Look beyond outputs to changes in safety, behavior and well-being. Intensity matters: regular engagement, not enrollment alone, is linked to better outcomes (Jay et al., 2026, and Jawa et al., 2026, cited in HVIP focus group brief, 2026).
- Create an evaluation plan with process and outcome measures, and include racial equity, since some data sources undercount community experiences (BJA Checklist, Step 5.3).
- Work with a research partner as an objective evaluator (BJA Checklist, Step 5.3).
- Build a learning agenda, and share findings through public reports and community events, not only academic papers (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).

## Guardrails

- Check data sources for bias before drawing conclusions (BJA Checklist, Steps 1.2 and 5.2).
- Do not attribute savings to a program just because violence declined; ROI needs a credible impact estimate (Dr. Roman, cost evaluation and ROI).
- Design data systems that fit frontline workflows. Staff strengths center on relationships, so keep documentation feasible (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).
- Protect privacy. Collect only what you need and store it securely.

## Team conversation starters

- Which evaluation readiness stage are we in right now?
- What one question about our work would we most like data to answer?
- How do we bring results back to the community?
