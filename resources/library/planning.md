# Planning (PL) field guide

Planning is how an organization turns its mission into a shared roadmap: goals, work plans, a strategy and a sense of where funding will come from. In the 2025 OCI cohort, Planning was the domain with the most room to grow, and it moved together with Fundraising and Financial Management, so progress here tends to lift other areas too (2025 OCI cohort findings).

## By stage

### Seeding and Emerging: write down what you already know

- Start with a simple roadmap, or logic model, that links your activities to the changes you expect. Add a short theory of change explaining why your approach works in your community (BJA CVI Implementation Checklist, Step 3.1).
- Base priorities on a community violence assessment and community input: which places, which types of violence and which groups you will focus on (BJA Checklist, Steps 1.2 and 3.1).
- Build the foundation before you grow. Put leadership, staffing roles, documentation, referral steps and data systems in place before expanding services (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).
- Match scope to capacity. Leaders advise choosing what you do based on your resources, not your emotions, because fast growth can lead to burnout and thin follow-up (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).

### Developing: make plans a working tool

- Turn goals into a 6 to 12 month action plan with every task, a deadline and the person responsible, built with key partners so nothing falls through the cracks (BJA Checklist, Step 3.2).
- Review progress against the plan on a regular rhythm, for example at monthly team meetings, and adjust as participant and community needs change (BJA Checklist, Step 3.2).
- Set short, clear milestones. A useful habit is asking what success looks like at 48 hours, 30 days and 90 days (Dr. Tillmon, slide 11).
- Name where funding for the next year will come from, and note any gaps early (OCI questionnaire, Planning).

### Strengthening and Transformational: plan for the long run

- Develop a 2 to 3 year strategic plan with a multi-year budget, capacity-building goals and a sustainability strategy, and connect it to yearly work plans (OCI questionnaire, Planning).
- Plan for sustainability early by building relationships with public, private and community champions (BJA Checklist, Step 6.1).
- Involve your board in setting direction, and use the plan to decide what you will not take on.
- Share your planning tools and lessons with peer organizations (BJA Checklist, Step 6.1).

## Practical tips

- Keep the first plan short. Two pages that the whole team can explain beats a long document nobody uses.
- Avoid letting each new grant reset your priorities. Grant chasing makes long-term planning hard (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).
- Plan staff coverage honestly. Response hours and caseloads should follow staffing, not the other way around (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).

## Team conversation starters

- Can every staff member describe our top three goals in their own words?
- What would we stop doing if we had to protect our core work?
- Who outside our organization should help shape our next plan?
