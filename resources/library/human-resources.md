# Human Resources (HR) field guide

Human Resources covers how an organization recruits, trains, supports and retains its people, especially staff from the community such as credible messengers, outreach workers and violence interrupters. Workforce strength was a bright spot for the 2025 cohort (2025 OCI cohort findings), and it is also where burnout risk is highest. Caring for staff is part of caring for participants.

## By stage

### Seeding and Emerging: clear roles and steady pay

- Recruit trusted, credible messengers from the community and pay them for their work, with training and professional support (BJA CVI Implementation Checklist, Steps 2.2 and 4.4).
- Value lived experience. Leaders say the job does not require a college degree; it requires people who can build relationships (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).
- Use fair chance hiring and plan ahead for considerations that come with hiring people who were involved in the justice system (BJA Checklist, Step 4.4).
- Write down each role and set up a simple onboarding process so new staff know what to expect (OCI questionnaire, Human Resources).

### Developing: supervision, wellness and structure

- Hold regular one-on-one supervision focused on support and growth, not only compliance.
- Treat wellness as part of operations, not an occasional benefit. Leaders recommend regular trauma education, including short live sessions such as lunch-and-learn trainings (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).
- Provide respite and trauma support, fair income and job security for outreach workers and interrupters (BJA Checklist, Step 4.4).
- Move staff from stipends to payroll when you can, and write job descriptions for every position (OCI questionnaire, Human Resources).
- Set caseloads and on-call hours by staffing levels. Round-the-clock coverage by a small team led to burnout in several programs; separating crisis response from follow-up case management helped (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).

### Strengthening and Transformational: careers and retention

- Build career ladders. Many CVI teams are "bottom-heavy," with few supervisor or specialist roles, so experienced staff have nowhere to grow (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).
- Start succession planning early, before experienced people are at risk of leaving (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).
- Offer competitive pay and benefits as capacity allows, adopt core HR policies, train supervisors and hold regular performance conversations (OCI questionnaire, Human Resources).
- Use staff feedback and turnover patterns to improve hiring and retention.

## What good leadership looks like

Strong CVI leaders combine relational skills, technical skills, management capacity (supervision, procedures, budgets, staff wellness), ethical discipline and systems thinking (Dr. Tillmon, slide 10). The License to Operate applies inside the organization too: consistency, integrity and results keep trust (Dr. Tillmon, slide 6).

## Funding the workforce

Funders often prefer direct-service positions, which makes supervisors, trainers and senior roles hard to pay for (HVIP focus group brief, 2026). Name these roles in budgets and proposals as part of service quality.

## Team conversation starters

- What helps our staff stay, and what pushes people out?
- Who supports the people who support participants?
- What is the next step up for a frontline worker here?
