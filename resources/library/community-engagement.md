# Community Engagement and Partnerships (CE) field guide

Community engagement and partnerships is how an organization builds relationships, communicates and collaborates with its community and partners. For most CVI organizations this is a deep strength: in 2025 the cohort felt similarly strong about its community ties (2025 OCI cohort findings). The next step is usually turning trusted relationships into coordinated, lasting partnerships.

## By stage

### Seeding and Emerging: trust first

- Be present where and when people gather. One leader put it simply: if people are out at 10 or 11 at night, that is where you need to be (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).
- Keep asking residents what they need and what they want. Leaders credit that habit with their staying power (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).
- Offer many ways to get involved, and watch for planning fatigue or skepticism (BJA CVI Implementation Checklist, Step 1.1).
- Pay community members and partners for their time and input (BJA Checklist, Step 4.1).

### Developing: coordinate with partners

- Map partners across public health, public agencies, community groups and private funders, and look for missing partners (BJA Checklist, Steps 2.1 and 2.3).
- Define shared goals and each partner's role, and repair past mistrust where needed (BJA Checklist, Step 2.2).
- Test reliability before deeper commitments. Formal agreements alone do not make a partnership work; trust and follow-through should come first. One program saw a failed partnership leave the community more distrusting (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).
- Value informal partners. Some of the most respected peer supporters are grassroots people with no formal affiliation (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).

### Strengthening and Transformational: lead the ecosystem

- Help build or join a local coalition or cross-sector advisory group to share information, spot service gaps and reduce competition for funding (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).
- Put people with lived experience in program delivery and leadership (OCI questionnaire, Community Engagement).
- Contribute to broader strategies and policy, and talk directly with legislators about what programs really need (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).
- Learn from peers in other places through peer-to-peer partnerships (BJA Checklist, Step 6.1).

## Working with justice partners

- Decide together what level of collaboration with law enforcement, courts and supervision is right, and how information will be shared, with attention to privacy and safety (BJA Checklist, Step 4.2).
- Keep roles distinct. When community practitioners are seen as part of enforcement, they can lose the trust that makes their work possible (Dr. Tillmon, slide 9).
- Focus on the shared client. Coordination with supervision agencies can ease requirements during a family crisis (HVIP focus group brief, 2026).

## Communication

Use several channels, such as community forums, social media and your website, to share progress and invite feedback, and build that feedback into a continuous improvement plan (BJA Checklist, Step 4.1).

## Team conversation starters

- Whose voices are we not hearing yet?
- Which partnership would most help our participants this year?
- Are our boundaries with justice partners clear to everyone?
